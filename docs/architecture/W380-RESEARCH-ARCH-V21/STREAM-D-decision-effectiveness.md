### Findings — concrete decision-model weaknesses (each tied to the verdict that exposes it)

**F1 — SCHEMA/PRACTICE GAP: zero verdicts actually use sca-v20.** The model under audit is sca-v20 (19-dim, per-class, Pareto), but every real decision used sca-v3.1 (#1), sca-v7 (#2), or NO rubric at all (#3–#6 deepwiki narratives). sca-v20's own `follow_up_streams` lists R-CALIBRATION (empirical weight tuning) and R-MIGRATION (backfill 15 repos) as **unexecuted**. *The decision model has never been run on a real decision.* This is the single largest effectiveness finding. (Exposed by: every row; schema `follow_up_streams` line 365.)

**F2 — PERSONA-VETO is non-reproducible and now redundant.** OpenHands (#1) was downgraded by an architect-persona "architectural-fit-block" overriding a T2-band scalar. v20's D13_cc_install_path hard-filter encodes the same outcome deterministically (`library-only/none` → cannot INSTALL). Keeping both means the same decision can be reached two ways with different audit trails. (Exposed by: #1.)

**F3 — AD-HOC MULTIPLIERS with no schema home.** haizelabs (#2) carries a `×0.85 deep-ingest-incomplete` factor that exists in no rubric version. Undocumented confidence-discounts let any analyst move a score across a tier boundary unchallenged. v20 *does* have `confidence`/`confidence_by_dimension` — the discount should live there, not in the scalar. (Exposed by: #2.)

**F4 — DEAD-WEIGHT DIMENSIONS: 19 declared, ~1–3 used.** The memory-candidate routing (#7) and every deepwiki narrative (#3–#6) decided tier almost entirely on **D13 install-path**. D06 issue-close-rate, D08 signed-release-level, D10 test-coverage, D11 CI-streak, D15 cite-anchor-density were declared-but-never-measured in any real verdict. A 19-dim model that decides on 1–3 dims is over-engineered relative to its evidence. (Exposed by: #3–#7.)

**F5 — MISSING SIGNAL #1: hosted-service / data-egress / token-custody.** The decisive factor for composio (#4) — "all tool calls + OAuth tokens egress to a third-party cloud" — is **not a v20 dimension**. For MCP-server candidates this is the dominant install-risk and it's invisible to the rubric; only prose caught it. (Exposed by: #4.)

**F6 — MISSING SIGNAL #2: dependency-blast-radius / runtime-weight.** neo4j-labs/agent-memory (#5) was correctly held back by its Neo4j 5.11+ dependency, but v20 has no transitive-dependency or heavy-runtime dimension (CLAUDE.md CR-1(d) names "dependency blast-radius" but it never made it into the schema). D13 alone would have mis-routed it to INSTALL. (Exposed by: #5.)

**F7 — INCONSISTENT THRESHOLDS across rule versions.** sca-v3.1 used a 16-dim scalar with floor ~3.5; sca-v7 used 33 dims with a 4.5 ship-gate; sca-v20 uses per-class [0,1] weighted sums. The same repo scored under different versions is not comparable, and there is no documented cross-version normalization beyond an informal "v6.1 verdicts ×0.9 under v7." Backtesting effectiveness is impossible without a frozen rubric. (Exposed by: #1 vs #2 vs #7.)

**F8 — CONVERGENCE-SOURCE vs ADOPTION-CANDIDATE role conflation.** microsoft/agent-framework (#6) and inspect_ai were used as *corroboration sources* for OTHER repos' patterns, then filed as "pattern-study" notes as if they were independently evaluated subjects. The model has no field distinguishing "this repo validated another decision" from "this repo was itself decided." (Exposed by: #6, and the W379 multi-source convergence section.)

**F9 — POSITIVE: stars-neutrality and the codex jury work.** Two things the model gets RIGHT: (a) OpenHands at 74k★ landed T3 — stars did not override structure (#1 anti-bias note), and v20 formalizes `stars=0 weight`; (b) the codex GPT-5.5 REVISE@0.84 gate (#8) caught contract-risk + a missing eval-gate the rubric entirely missed. **Implication: the cross-model jury is currently carrying the decision quality the rubric is failing to encode.** Calibration should aim to *move the jury's catches into the rubric*, not replace the jury.

### PART 1 verdict
The decision MODEL (sca-v20) is well-designed on paper (Pareto per-class, stars-neutral, cite-anchored) but **effectively unproven**: it has never scored a real adoption. Real decisions were made by older scalar rubrics + persona/codex judgment. The outcomes have generally been *sound* (no obviously-wrong tier found), but the *consistency and reproducibility* are weak — driven by un-encoded vetoes (F2), ad-hoc multipliers (F3), single-dimension routing (F4), and two missing signals that actually decided cases (F5, F6). **Top priority for v21: (1) run the rubric on the existing 12-repo corpus as a real backtest (R-MIGRATION); (2) add data-egress + dependency-weight dims; (3) retire/down-rank the dead-weight dims or make them measured-required; (4) encode the codex jury's recurring catches (contract-change, missing-eval-gate) as hard-filters.**

### PART 2 verdict
Top 3 additions (deps.dev, OSV.dev, OSSF Scorecard) are **all free, no-auth, CLASS-A-grade, and confirmed-live**, and they directly close the two missing-signal findings (F5 partially via Scorecard token-permissions; F6 via deps.dev dependency graph) plus harden the CR-1 trust-tuple CVE/signed-release checks. They should be added as a new **CLASS-A "package-intelligence" sub-cluster** in the convergence model. GitHub GraphQL (#5) is the highest-leverage *efficiency* fix — it makes running the full 19-dim vector cheap enough to actually do (addressing F4). Semantic Scholar + arXiv (#6, #7) turn academic-authority prose into measurable D06/D19 inputs. Sourcegraph (#8) is the optional usage/duplication signal.
